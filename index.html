Research project

Universal Test-Time Adaptation for Speech Enhancement Dec 2025 - Aug 2026

Institute for Natural Language Processing (IMS) in collaboration with Signal Processing and System Theory (ISS), University of Stuttgart
Supervisor: Tobias Raichle  |  Examiner: Dr. Wolfgang Wokurek (IMS, Digital Phonetics)

CMGANPyTorchSpeech EnhancementTTADereverberation

Speech enhancement models work well in controlled settings but struggle when deployed in the real world, where acoustic conditions vary wildly. Test-time adaptation (TTA) lets a model adapt on the fly to new conditions, without labelled data at inference time. I trained a model on mixed noisy and reverberant speech, then adapted it on one distortion and tested it on the other, to see whether the two share characteristics a model can exploit.

I trained four CMGAN models (noise-only, reverb-only, and two trained jointly on both) and evaluated four TTA methods, including RIRmixIT, a reverb-specific method developed for the project. The existing methods (MPol, LaDen, RemixIT) all degrade performance on reverberation, because their adaptation assumptions hold for additive noise but not for convolutive distortion. Weight-level analysis showed that updates concentrate in the shared conformer blocks' normalisation statistics, which are only useful for adaptation after joint training.

Central result: adapting on one distortion gave larger gains on the other than adapting on it directly. The full numbers are in the report.

Projects

Voclair: Privacy-first Speech Enhancement Oct 2026 - Current

Voclair is built for lecture halls, conference venues and live events, where noise, echo and reverberation spoil speech. It grew out of my research project on how noisy and reverberant speech are linked, and won the Best Pitch Award at the Cyber Valley AI Incubator Demo Day.

The live demo has a ready-made sample you can hear with and without noise. You can also upload your own recording: it is denoised and you get the cleaned speech back. Everything runs in JavaScript inside your browser, so no audio is sent to a server and nothing is stored on our side. The audio stays with you, and privacy is the main point of the product. Real-time operation is the next goal.

WorldSounds (Team Sundarsheng): Cross-Tradition Music Intelligence Jul 2026

Built at the MTG Generative Music AI Workshop (Universitat Pompeu Fabra, Barcelona). A knowledge base of Carnatic and Chinese Jingju (Beijing opera) features, covering CREPE pitch tracks, gamaka/meend scores and pentatonic adherence, drives a 2nd-order Markov chain melody generator. The goal was to turn musicological insight about two very different traditions into something a model can generate from.
